The honest part

Building from abroad isn't a distance problem.
It's a trust problem.

Almost every diaspora build stalls the same way. The money is real, the intentions are good, and nobody with authority is standing on the site.

01

The money finished. The house didn't.

Tranche two cleared, the site went quiet, and no one could say exactly what the money touched.

02

The photos always look good.

A camera can make a weak mix look like progress. Nobody was checking the specification, the quantities or the curing.

03

Your relative means well. He isn't an engineer.

Family can watch a site. Reading a bill of quantities, testing concrete and holding retention is a different job.

04

Then comes the diaspora price.

The moment a contractor hears the client is abroad, the same cement costs a third more. Nobody is negotiating for you.

05

The argument you cannot win.

Delays, "extras", a threat to down tools. You argue on WhatsApp across time zones, and he can simply wait you out.

06

Approvals nobody warned you about.

Title, cession, council plans, EMA, ZINWA, ZESA. One missed gate stops the entire build while it is explained to you.

None of that is a paperwork problem. It's a presence problem. Somebody has to stand on that site, hold the money and take the calls. That somebody is us.
